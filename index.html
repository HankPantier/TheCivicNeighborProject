The Golden Rule + The Preamble

Treat each other well. Build a more perfect Union.

Two promises most Americans already share. The Golden Rule asks us to treat others the way we want to be treated. The Preamble asks us to form “a more perfect Union.” We start there.

On November 8th, we’ll still be neighbors.

However the votes fall, the people in the same pews, grocery aisles and hometown bleachers will still be our neighbors the morning after. Let’s treat each other that way now.

Why now

A nation challenged by division.

People who sit in the same pews, shop at the same grocery stores, and cheer for the same hometown teams now view one another with suspicion because of politics and culture wars. Many Americans feel the isolation but struggle to name it.

We start from a simple belief: a healthy nation depends on staying connected to one another, even when we disagree.

The core of our mission

One teaches consideration; the other teaches connection.

Our missionWe call Americans to practice a shared civic ethic rooted in the Golden Rule and the Preamble to the U.S. Constitution to build a more compassionate, connected and resilient nation.

Our moral commitment

The Golden Rule

Treat others the way we want to be treated. Shared across nearly every religious, secular, and philosophical tradition, it calls us to dignity, fairness, restraint, and care.

Our civic commitment

The Preamble

Unite for justice, peace, security, opportunity, and liberty for all Americans. It reminds us what we owe our neighbors and our shared future.

The question we bring to every public debate

What do the Golden Rule and the Preamble call us to do in this moment?

What we do

Building a healthier civic culture.

Through our Substack, podcasts, social media, articles, public letters, educational resources and conversations, we respond to public debates with compassion and moral reasoning. We:

  • Highlight bridge-building across generations, regions, communities, and cultures.
  • Encourage respectful engagement across political and ideological differences.
  • Support neighbors and organizations who model consideration and connection.
  • Demonstrate that disagreement can happen without dehumanization, even with love.
  • Remind Americans that belonging is essential to democratic life.
  • Cultivate service and leadership grounded in the Golden Rule and the Preamble.

The Neighbor Papers

Short essays for a more perfect Union.

In 1787, Alexander Hamilton, James Madison and John Jay wrote a series of short newspaper essays, now known as the Federalist Papers, to make the case for the new Constitution to their fellow citizens. The Neighbor Papers follow their example: plain-spoken essays from our working groups, each asking what the Golden Rule and the Preamble call us to do now.

Who we are

Neighbors first.

We began in the summer of 2026 as a small circle of retired military leaders, clergy, educators, public servants, civic leaders and community volunteers. We come from different backgrounds and reached the same conclusion: our nation needs more spaces where people can disagree without becoming enemies.
